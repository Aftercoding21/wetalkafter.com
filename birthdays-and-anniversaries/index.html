Grief

Birthdays and anniversaries without them.

Some days in the year are heavier than others. Their birthday. The day they died. Mother’s Day or Father’s Day. The calendar keeps bringing them back, and each time it can feel like the first. If one of these days is coming, you are not alone.

The days before

For many people, the days leading up to the date are harder than the day itself. A sadness settles in, sometimes before you even notice the date is near. You may also feel tired, irritable or low without knowing why.

This is your heart remembering. It is a normal part of grief.

Their birthday

Their birthday is still their day. Some people bake a cake, light a candle, cook their favourite meal or visit a place they loved. Some buy a present they would have loved, or give one to someone in their name. Or write them a few words.

Others prefer to let the day pass quietly. Both are ways of remembering.

The anniversary of their death

Your body and mind may remember the days before they died, even years later. Images, sounds or the weather can bring it back.

If memories of that day come back, you don’t have to push them away. Let them come, and let them pass, a little like waves. And alongside that day, you can also remember their life: the laughter, the ordinary days, who they were.

Mother’s Day, Father’s Day and other days

Shops, adverts and social media can be full of cards and family photos. It can hurt, whether you have lost a mum, a dad or a child.

It is OK to step away from your phone, change your plans, spend the day with people who understand, or spend it on your own.

Planning the day, a little

  • Decide in advance what you would like to do, even if it is very simple.
  • Tell one person that this day is hard for you.
  • Plan a small tribute: a candle, a song, a walk, a letter.
  • Allow yourself an easy way out if plans become too much.
  • It is also OK to spend the day alone, or to do nothing at all.

Questions

Will these days always hurt this much?

For many people, the dates become gentler over time, even if they never become ordinary. Some years will be harder than others, and that is normal too.

Is it OK to celebrate their birthday?

Yes. Celebrating them, laughing at a memory or sharing their favourite cake are ways of keeping them close. There is no wrong way to mark the day.

What can I say to a friend on one of these days?

A short message is enough: “I’m thinking of you and of [their name] today.” Saying their name shows you remember them too. You don’t have to find the perfect words.
