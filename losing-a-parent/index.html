Grief

Losing a parent.

Losing your mum or dad changes the ground under your feet. Whether it was sudden or after a long illness, whether you were close or it was complicated, whether you are 15 or 60, you have lost one of the people who were there from the very beginning. If you are here, you are not alone.

When it comes back without warning

Sometimes it’s a small thing. You reach for the phone, you smell their perfume in a shop, or you find their handwriting on an old card.

Grief can catch you by surprise, months and even years later. It is a sign of how present they were in your life.

When it was complicated

Not every relationship with a parent was easy. You can miss them and still carry what was hard between you. You may also grieve what never was, the conversation you didn’t have, the parent you needed.

Grief can hold all of this at the same time. There is no right way to feel.

After a long illness

If you cared for them, you may have been grieving long before they died: the person they were before the illness, the roles that changed.

When it ends, there can be exhaustion, emptiness, sometimes relief, and guilt for feeling relief. All of this is human.

When the family changes

Losing a parent changes the whole family. You may find yourself supporting the parent who is left, or your brothers and sisters, while you are grieving too.

If you are an only child, you may carry it all on your own, and feel that no one else remembers your childhood the way they did. If both parents are gone, it can feel like there is no longer a home to go back to.

Your grief needs space as well.

What can help, a little

  • Keep a photo of them where you can see it every day.
  • Keep their voice close: a voice message, a video, a letter in their handwriting.
  • Cook their recipes, wear something of theirs, play their music.
  • Take your time with their things. There is no rush to sort or decide.
  • Tell their stories to your children, your friends, anyone who wants to listen.
  • Talk with others who have lost a mum or dad. They will understand without explanations.

Questions

Is it normal to still feel this, years later?

Yes. Grief for a parent doesn’t follow a calendar. A song, a smell or a big moment in your life can bring it back. It is a sign of the place they had in your life.

How can I support my other parent while I am grieving too?

You don’t have to be strong for everyone. Share what you can, let other people help, and keep a little space for your own grief.

What can I say to a friend who has lost a parent?

Say “I’m so sorry. I’m here.” Ask about their mum or dad, and listen to the stories. Remember the hard dates, like birthdays or Mother’s and Father’s Day, and keep asking how they are, months later.
