Our story

Why I created After

My story, and the reason behind this community.

This story talks about the sudden death of my brother. Read it when it feels right.

I think I’ve been afraid of death for as long as I can remember.

I was about seven years old when I first saw someone I loved lying in a coffin. It was my grandfather.

I remember looking at him and wondering, What’s happening? Where did he go? How can someone just stop being here?

That was the moment I understood that the people we love don’t live forever.

And I couldn’t accept it.

As a little girl, I would pray to God, to the universe, to whoever might be listening, asking them to let my family live for a hundred years.

But then I would think, A hundred years will end, too.

So I would ask for a thousand years.

And then I realized that a thousand years would also end.

So I asked for forever.

Growing up, our family life wasn’t always easy. We had difficult moments, like many families do. But through everything, my greatest fear was always the same: losing someone I loved.

Then, on January 15, 2003, my greatest fear became my reality.

My little brother Charles died in an accident. He was only 21.

I found out late that night. He had no identification with him, and I had to search for him. I had to face the unimaginable reality of finding his body, calling my parents to tell them their son was gone, and bringing my brother’s body home.

That night, I didn’t know how I could possibly go on living.

My world had stopped.

For the next three years, I cried more than I ever thought a human being could cry.

I read book after book, searching for answers. Trying to understand life, death, love, and why we are given these extraordinary feelings for one another if, one day, everything can suddenly end.

I wanted to make sense of something that made no sense to me.

And as the years passed, something else happened.

People stopped asking.

Some thought I should be feeling better by then. Others didn’t know what to say. And eventually, I stopped talking about it.

Not because I had stopped hurting.

But because I felt there was no longer room for my grief.

Even twenty years later, when I allowed myself to think deeply about losing Charles, the shock could feel as fresh as if it had happened yesterday.

I could feel the panic, the disbelief, the tragedy of that night all over again.

Eventually, I knew I needed help.

I tried EMDR therapy. It isn’t the answer for everyone, but it helped me begin to approach memories I had spent years trying not to touch.

And something changed.

I found that I could finally talk about my brother a little more.

And I wanted to.

I wanted to talk about him, about what happened, about everything I had felt, and everything I still didn’t understand.

But with whom?

People have their own lives, their own problems. You don’t always want to call a friend and bring sadness into their day.

So you keep it inside.

And I started thinking: How many people around the world are doing exactly the same thing?

How many people have a story they desperately want to tell, but no one they feel comfortable telling it to?

When I first imagined After, I considered using artificial intelligence to offer support.

And AI can be incredibly helpful. It can explain our emotions, help us understand what we’re experiencing, and find the right words when we can’t.

But I realized something was missing for me.

I didn’t just want someone to explain my grief. I wanted someone who understood it because they had lived through something similar.

Someone who could say, “I know what you mean,” and truly mean it.

Someone who wouldn’t be uncomfortable hearing about my brother for the hundredth time.

Someone who might actually want to talk about their own brother, mother, father, child, partner, or friend, too.

And that’s when After became clear to me.

A community built around human connection.

A place where people can share their experiences, with just a first name or a username, without judgment, without pressure to feel better, and without worrying that they’re making someone else’s day sad.

And not only about death.

Because life can change in so many ways. Divorce, illness, separation, losing a sense of who we are, or having to start again when the future we imagined disappears.

We all experience moments that divide our lives into a before and an after.

And sometimes the person who can help us feel less alone is someone we’ve never met, somewhere in the world, who has been through something similar.

I created After because I wish something like it had existed when I lost Charles.

And because, 23 years later, I still want to talk about my brother.

Not because I’m unable to move forward. But because he was here. He mattered. I loved him. And I always will.

I believe there are millions of people who feel the same way about someone, or something, they’ve lost.

And maybe, if we find each other, the journey won’t feel quite so lonely.

That’s why I created After.

Real people. Real stories. A place to be heard.

Sandra, founder of After
