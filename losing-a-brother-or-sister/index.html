Grief

Losing a brother or sister.

When a brother or sister dies, you lose part of your past and part of your future: the person who has known you all your life, and who you thought would be there for the rest of it. If you are living this, you are not alone.

The one who shared your story

A brother or sister is often your first friend. The only other person who grew up in the same house, who knows the family jokes, who you could talk to about your parents.

When they are gone, there is no one left who remembers it quite the way you do.

The forgotten grief

People often ask how your parents are coping, and forget to ask about you. Some call siblings the “forgotten mourners”.

You may find yourself holding it together for everyone else, with no space left for your own grief. Your loss counts too.

What it can feel like

  • Guilt, about an argument, about the times you didn’t call, or about simply still being here.
  • Anger, at what happened, or at the world carrying on as if nothing had changed.
  • A new place in the family: becoming an only child, or the eldest, overnight.
  • Their birthday, and later, growing older than they ever were.

All of this is part of grief. There is no right way to feel.

Small things that can help

  • Say their name, and ask the people around you to say it too.
  • Keep something of theirs close: a jumper, a song, a photo.
  • Find someone who knew them as you did, and share the family stories.
  • Mark their birthday in your own way, quietly or with others.
  • Talk to someone who has lost a brother or sister. They will understand without explanations.

Questions

Will it always hurt this much?

Many people say the pain changes with time. It doesn’t disappear, but it becomes something you learn to carry, and the love stays.

How do I talk about them when people stop asking?

It is common for people to stop asking after a few months. You can still talk about them: to friends who knew them, to family, or to people who have lived the same loss.

Is it normal to feel guilty?

Yes. Guilt is one of the most common feelings after losing a sibling. It doesn’t mean you did something wrong. It is often love with nowhere to go.
