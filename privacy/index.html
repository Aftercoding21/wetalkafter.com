Privacy policy

Last updated [date]

After is a place for people who have lost someone, or whose life changed when something ended. What you share here is often the most private thing you have. This page says plainly what we keep, why, for how long, and what you can do about it.

In short

  • No AI reads your conversations or learns from what you share.
  • No tracking, and we never sell your data. Simple usage counts through “Help improve After”, which you can switch off.
  • Your memories are private. Only you see them, unless you share one.
  • Group messages are deleted 15 minutes after each meeting ends.
  • We never store your mobile number, only a scrambled code of it.
  • A person on our team reads messages only when someone reports them or taps “I’m worried about them”.
  • You can delete your account in the app at any time, and everything that’s yours goes with it.

What we keep, and why

When you sign up

  • Your email address and password, to sign you in and to send a link if you forget your password. Your password is stored scrambled; we can’t read it.
  • Your username. This is the only name others see. No photos.
  • Your loss, if you choose to give it (who or what, and roughly when). It helps match you with people in a similar place. You can leave it out.
  • That you said you’re 18 or older.
  • A scrambled code of your mobile number. We send your number to our text-message provider to send you a 6-digit code, then keep only a code made from it that can’t be turned back into the number. It’s used for one thing: keeping banned accounts out.
  • A random code for your phone. The app makes it the first time it opens. It says nothing about you or your phone. We use it to ask for the texted code again when you sign in on a new phone, and to limit new accounts to 3 per phone a month (we keep a scrambled copy for 30 days).
  • A quick human check. When you sign up or sign in, Cloudflare checks that you’re a person and not a program. It looks at signals from your browser or phone, never at what you write. See “Keeping After human” below.

When you use After

  • Groups: the groups you start or book, your place on a waiting list, and the messages written in them. Messages are deleted for everyone 15 minutes after the meeting ends. The group itself is deleted a day later. If you host a meeting and invite a guest, we keep the invitation code and who used it; the group sees who the guest came with.
  • Talk: who you met, whether you said you’d talk again (nobody else sees your answer), your starred people, your private chats, whether you’ve switched on “I’m around to talk”, and requests to talk.
  • Remember: your spaces and memories: writing, photos, videos, voice notes and files, with the words and dates you add. Only you can see them. If you share one memory with someone in Your people, they can see that one. If you make a private link, anyone with the link can see that one memory until you stop the link.
  • Safety: reports, with the messages they’re about; conversations flagged with “I’m worried about them”; people you’ve hidden; removals and bans from a group; and our decisions, warnings, suspensions and appeals.
  • Ideas and problems you send us from “An idea or a problem?”, our answer, and whether you said we may reply.
  • Volunteering: if you offer to help, what you wrote and our decision.
  • Coming back: when you write in a chat or a group, we note the week, with your account, so we can count how many people come back the week after. The team only ever sees weekly totals.
  • “Did this help you feel understood?” Your answer is kept without your account or name, so it can’t be linked to you. We only note, with your account, when you were last asked, so it’s at most once a week.
  • Help improve After (you can switch it off): it’s on for new accounts, and you can switch it off in Settings at any time. While it’s on, the app counts a few moments: a chat started, a group joined or not (with a fixed reason, like “group full”), a Calm exercise started, finished or stopped before the end, a sound that couldn’t play, and the app crashing. Each count holds only the moment, the day and a random code so a retry isn’t counted twice. Never your account, your name, who you talk to, your groups, your memories, your messages or anything you type. Our database checks with your account that it’s on, but doesn’t store who sent a count. The counts stay in our own database (Supabase, London), not with an analytics company. The team sees totals for 7 or 30 days, rounded to 5, with anything under 5 shown as “fewer than 5”. Switching it off stops it straight away.
  • After+: whether you said you’d like After+ when it’s ready.

Keeping After human

After is only for real people, so we check, quietly, that nobody is a program or an AI pretending to be a person:

  • Cloudflare’s human check runs when you sign up, sign in, and before you start talking (at most about once a day). We keep the date and time you last passed it.
  • Signs of a machine. The app notices how a message was typed: if a long message appears all at once, or keys are pressed at a machine-perfect pace. Only that sign is sent to us, never your keystrokes or timings. We also note very fast long replies, many messages in a minute, and activity at almost every hour of the day. We never look at what you write for this.
  • When several signs add up in a day, you’re simply asked to do the human check again, and a person on our team may look at the account. Nobody is banned automatically for these signs. They’re deleted after 30 days.

If you ask us to tell you when After opens

Your email address and the country you chose on wetalkafter.com. We use it for one thing: to let you know when After opens where you live. We may also write to you if there’s something important you need to know about After or your information. We don’t send promotional emails. You can ask us to delete it at any time.

On your phone

The app keeps a few small things on your phone so it works smoothly: that you’re signed in, which country’s crisis lines to show, the tab and space you were last on, and your group reminders (made by your phone itself, at the times of the groups you booked). They don’t leave your phone, apart from the random phone code described above.

What we don’t collect

No location, no contacts, no advertising IDs, no outside analytics or tracking tools, no screen recordings or screenshots, no photos of you. The microphone and your photos are only used when you choose to record a voice note or add a photo.

Sensitive information

Grief touches everything, so what you write may reveal things about your health, beliefs, relationships or family. We treat everything you share in After as sensitive. We only use it to run After for you and to keep people safe, and we ask for your explicit consent to this when you sign up. You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your account.

No AI, and no selling

No AI writes to you, reads your chats or memories, or learns from them. Danger is spotted with a short list of words we wrote ourselves: if you write one, the app shows you a crisis line, and nobody else is told. We never sell your information, never use it for advertising, and never let the companies we work with use it to train AI.

Like many small teams, we used AI tools to help design and build the app itself. That never involved anything you share.

Who at After can read what

A person on our team reads messages only when:

  • someone reports them (the messages around the reported one, from everyone in that conversation, are attached to the report, including yours if you were part of it), or
  • someone taps “I’m worried about them”, which flags that conversation, or
  • someone reports a memory that was shared with them.

We don’t read anything else: not your memories, not your private chats, not your groups.

Deleting your account

In the app, go to Settings → Delete my account. Your account, username, loss, spaces, memories and files, shares, links, places in groups and your people are deleted straight away. In past chats and groups, your messages stay for the others, shown as from “Someone who left”. Before you delete, you can save all your memories in one file from Settings → Save all my memories.

Your rights

You can ask us to:

  • give you a copy of the information we hold about you;
  • correct anything that’s wrong;
  • delete your information (you can also do this yourself in the app);
  • give you your information in a form you can take elsewhere;
  • stop or limit using it, or object to how we use it.

Write to privacy@wetalkafter.com. It’s free, and we’ll reply within one month.

If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your information, please tell us first. You can also complain to the data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk); in France, the CNIL (cnil.fr); in Ireland, the Data Protection Commission (dataprotection.ie); in Brazil, the ANPD (gov.br/anpd).

Keeping it safe

Everything is sent encrypted. Rules in our database mean each person can only reach their own information, and we tested them so that, for example, nobody else can open your memories. Files are shown through links that stop working after an hour. Only a small number of people on our team can read reports, and nobody can read anything else.

Age

After is for people aged 18 or over. If we learn that someone under 18 has an account, we close it and delete their information.

This website

wetalkafter.com uses no analytics and no tracking. It’s protected by Cloudflare, which may set a small security cookie to tell people from bots; it’s never used for advertising or to follow you elsewhere. If you leave your email to hear when After opens, it goes straight into our own database in London, not to a mailing-list company. Its fonts are served from the site itself, so no other company learns that you visited. If you open a memory someone shared with you, the page fetches only that one memory.

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll tell you in the app before it takes effect. The date at the top shows when it last changed.
